Leading 1.6 reads more of what an Obsidian vault holds, lets you write notes on your highlights, and can read aloud.

- **Notes on highlights.** Click a highlight and choose ✎ to write a few words on it. A small dot marks a highlight with a note; click it to read the note. The note is kept in the file straight after the highlight, as an Obsidian comment (`==words==%%note%%`), so Obsidian hides it too. Show Highlights lists notes under their highlights, and Copy All Highlights includes them.
- **Links to this note.** View › Show Links to This Note (⌥⌘B) lists, in the margin, every note in the vault that links here, with the line where it does. Click one to open it.
- **Block links.** `[[Note#^id]]` opens a note at the paragraph, list item or table named `^id`, and marks it for a moment. `![[Note#^id]]` embeds just that block, and link previews show it.
- **Comments and tags.** `%%comments%%`, within a line or across paragraphs, no longer show on the page, in print or in Quick Look. `#tags` are shown as quiet labels.
- **Read Aloud.** View › Read Aloud (⌥⌘R) reads from the top of the window, or from the selection, in your Mac's Spoken Content voice, marking each word and keeping it in view. ⌥⌘R again stops.
- **Pictures full window.** Click a picture or a diagram to see it as large as the window allows; click again or press Esc to return.
- **Look Up.** Select a word and press ⌃⌘D for the dictionary.
- **A note in its own language.** Add `lang: de`, or any language code, to a note's properties, and Leading hyphenates it, and checks its spelling while you edit, by that language's rules.
- **Highlights take whole words.** A highlight that starts or ends partway through a word takes the whole word, so marking a passage never shifts the text around it.
- **Smaller fixes.** Callouts are set ragged right, with their list markers inside, and the table view's buttons stay below the title bar.
