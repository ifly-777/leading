If macOS blocks Leading

Leading is free, and is not signed with Apple’s paid developer certificate. So the first time you open it, macOS stops it and says it could not verify it. Letting it through takes a minute, and you only do it once: updates do not ask again.

  1. 1

    Open Leading, then click Done

    First drag Leading into the Applications folder, and open it from there. macOS shows the message on the right.

    Click Done. Not Move to Trash.

    L
    “Leading” Not Opened
    Apple could not verify “Leading” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.
    Done Move to Trash
  2. 2

    Open Anyway, in System Settings

    Open System Settings from the Apple menu, and choose Privacy & Security in the sidebar.

    Scroll down to Security. Beside “Leading” was blocked to protect your Mac, click Open Anyway.

    Security
    “Leading” was blocked to protect your Mac.Open Anyway
  3. 3

    Confirm it’s you

    macOS asks once more. Click Open Anyway, and confirm with Touch ID or the password you use to log in to your Mac.

    Leading opens. From now on it opens like any other app.

    L
    Open “Leading”?
    Apple is not able to verify that it is free from malware. Open it only if you trust where it came from.
    Open Anyway Move to Trash

Why this happens

Apple charges developers a yearly fee to have their apps verified. Leading is a free, one-person project, so it is signed without that certificate. macOS treats any such app this way.

Is it safe?

Leading’s source code is public, so anyone can read what it does. It has no account, collects nothing and sends nothing. Download it only from its GitHub page, below.
